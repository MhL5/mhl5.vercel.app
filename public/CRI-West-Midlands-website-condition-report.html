Executive summary

The site scores 30 out of 100 against a 55-point best-practice checklist for a local lead-generation site (47 checks could be verified; the rest are excluded rather than counted as failures).

The foundations are better than the score suggests. It runs on Next.js with server-rendered content and a Sanity CMS, the URLs are clean, and it holds unusually strong raw material: 31 detailed customer reviews with photos, real projects in 15 local towns, and specific performance figures (U-values of 0.15 and 0.18, fitting times of 1 to 5 days).

The problem is that almost none of this is visible to Google or to a homeowner in the first few seconds. Every page shares the same title and description, the homepage has no H1, the quote form has no URL, the phone number can’t be tapped, and the reviews and 10-year guarantee are hidden on inner pages. Most fixes are configuration and content work on the existing stack, not a rebuild.

#1 Critical Every page uses the same title tag: “CRI West Midlands” Technical SEOEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

All seven pages fetched return the identical title CRI West Midlands: /, /services/tiled-conservatory-roof, /services/interior-roof-insulation, /projects, /reviews, /faqs, /policies/privacy-policy.

Why it matters

The title is the blue link people click in Google and the strongest on-page relevance signal. None of the titles mentions “conservatory roof”, “tiled”, “insulation” or a town, so the site can realistically only rank for its own name. Identical titles also push Google to treat pages as near-duplicates and pick one to show.

Recommended fix

Give each page its own 50–60 character title. Suggested starting points:
PageSuggested title
/Tiled Conservatory Roofs & Insulation | CRI West Midlands
/services/tiled-conservatory-roofTiled Conservatory Roof Replacement | CRI West Midlands
/services/interior-roof-insulationConservatory Roof Insulation, Fitted in 1 Day | CRI
/projectsConservatory Roof Projects in Dudley & Stourbridge | CRI
/reviewsCRI West Midlands Reviews from 31 Local Customers
/faqsConservatory Roof FAQs: Cost, Planning, Guarantees | CRI
Better still, add seoTitle and seoDescription fields to the Sanity schema so the client can edit them, and return them from generateMetadata().

#2 High Phone number and email are plain text, not tap-to-call or mailto links UX & conversionEffort: LowLikely

Evidence

“07941 042 047” and “criwestmidlands@gmail.com” appear as plain text in the footer of all seven pages; the Facebook and Instagram URLs next to them are rendered as links, so the contrast is clear. The phone number does not appear in the header.

Why it matters

Most homeowners will browse on a phone. For a trades business, calls are often the biggest lead source, and every extra step (copying, switching apps) loses some of them.

Recommended fix

Wrap both in links, add the number to the header, and add a slim sticky “Call / Get a quote” bar on mobile.
#3 High “Get a quote” and “Make an enquiry” open a form that has no URL UX & conversionEffort: MediumConfirmed

Evidence

On all seven pages, “Get a quote”, “Get a free quote” and “Make an enquiry” are rendered without an href. The footer copy asks visitors to use “the form linked below”, but nothing is linked. There is no /contact or /get-a-quote page.

Why it matters

Without a URL, the quote form can’t be linked from Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, ads or emails; it can’t be measured as a goal in GA4; it can’t rank for searches like “conservatory roof quote”; and AI assistants or browsing agents can’t find it. This is the one page that turns visits into revenue.

Recommended fix

Create /get-a-quote with the form as a normal page. Keep the modal as an enhancement by making every CTA a real link that the modal intercepts. On success, route to /get-a-quote/thank-you so conversions can be counted. Add a short version of the form (name, phone, postcode, roof type) to the page itself.
#4 High 31 detailed reviews exist, but the homepage shows none of them UX & conversionEffort: MediumConfirmed

Evidence

/reviews lists 31 written reviews from Google, Facebook and Yell, many with photos. The homepage shows only an unlinked “25+ 5-star reviews” label (twice). The client concern “no social proof” is partly right: the proof exists, but it isn’t where buyers make the decision.

Why it matters

A roof conversion is a four-figure, high-trust purchase from a small firm. Seeing a real customer say “best thing we’ve ever done” beside the quote button does more for conversion than any design change. The counter also undersells the business (“25+” versus 31 on the page).

Recommended fix

Add a review band under the hero: three short quotes with first name, project type and source, a live Google rating and count from the Google Business Profile, and a link to /reviews. Update the counter to the real number, or pull it from Sanity so it can’t drift.
#5 High No location pages, though the site already has projects in 15 towns On-page SEOEffort: HighConfirmed

Evidence

Image alt text on /projects names Dudley, Sedgley, Kinver, Kingswinford, Brierley Hill, Wordsley, Wall Heath, Stourbridge, Gornal, Kidderminster, Stourton, Quarry Bank, Wombourne, Bobbington and Bewdley. No page targets any of them. Competitor lmdconservatoryroofs.co.uk has town pages including /conservatory-roof-stourbridge/, /conservatory-roof-dudley/, /conservatory-roof-kidderminster/ and /conservatory-roof-bewdley/, which is CRI’s home patch.

Why it matters

Most searches for this service include a town (“conservatory roof Stourbridge”). With no matching page, CRI is absent from those results while an out-of-area competitor occupies them.

Recommended fix

Build 6–8 area pages around real local jobs: photos, the customer’s review, the roof type, the timeframe and a map of the area served. Start with Stourbridge, Dudley, Kingswinford, Brierley Hill and Kidderminster. Avoid boilerplate pages with only the town name swapped; Google treats those as doorway pages and may ignore them.
#6 High One 40-character meta description is reused on every page On-page SEOEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

All seven pages share <meta name="description" content="Conservatory Roof Insulation Specialists"> (40 characters). It doesn’t mention tiled roofs, the guarantee, the free quote or the area served.

Why it matters

The description is the sales copy under the link in Google. At 40 characters Google will usually replace it with a random snippet from the page, so CRI loses control of its first impression on every search.

Recommended fix

Write a unique 140–160 character description per page that states the service, the outcome, a proof point and the action. Examples:
  • Home: Insulated tiled conservatory roofs and interior roof insulation across the West Midlands. Family-run since 2003, 10-year guarantee. Get a free quote.
  • Interior insulation: Interior conservatory roof insulation fitted in one day. Warmer in winter, cooler in summer (U-value 0.18). Free, no-obligation quote.
  • Reviews: Read 31 reviews from West Midlands homeowners who turned a too-hot, too-cold conservatory into a room they use all year round.
#7 High Homepage and service pages have no H1 heading On-page SEOEffort: LowLikely

Evidence

In the fetched HTML, the first heading on / is an H2 reading “CRI West Midlands”; both service pages also start at H2. /reviews, /projects, /faqs and the privacy policy do have an H1, so the templates support it. Confirm in view-source.

Why it matters

The H1 tells Google and screen-reader users what a page is about. On the homepage the only top-level heading is the brand name, which says nothing about conservatory roofs.

Recommended fix

Promote the service headline to H1 and demote the brand name to plain text. One H1 per page, describing the page’s topic.
#8 High Guarantee, CPA membership and track record are buried on service pages UX & conversionEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

“10 year guarantee… insurance-backed members of the CPA” appears only inside bullet lists on the two service pages. “Formed in 2003” is in a paragraph further down the homepage. A directory profile (FreeIndex) says the founder has over 30 years in the conservatory trade; the site doesn’t say this at all. No logos or badges anywhere.

Why it matters

Competitors put accreditations up front (NJN shows a FENSA badge; LMD states it is a Guardian Warm Roof accredited installer). Homeowners comparing three quotes use these as shortcuts for “will this firm still be around if something goes wrong”.

Recommended fix

Add a trust strip directly under the hero: “Family-run since 2003”, “10-year insurance-backed guarantee”, “CPA member”, Google rating. Use the CPA logo only if membership terms allow it.
#9 High No address or base town, a Gmail address and a mobile-only number UX & conversionEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

None of the seven pages gives a postal address or base town; the only location is “West Midlands”. Directory listings place the business in Stourbridge (FreeIndex profile: DY8 1SE; the Yell listing linked from /reviews is “cri-west-midlands-ltd-stourbridge”). Contact email is @gmail.com.

Why it matters

Local search ranking leans on consistent name, address and phone across the web, and on proximity. Buyers also read a named base and a business email address as signs of a real, established firm; a Gmail address reads as a sole trader working from a phone.

Recommended fix

Add name, address (or base town plus service area, matching the Google Business Profile setting) and phone to the footer and to structured data, exactly as they appear on GBP, Yell and FreeIndex. Set up an address on the domain, such as hello@criwestmidlands.co.uk.
#10 High FAQ answers are missing from the page HTML Technical SEOEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

/faqs returns 12 questions and zero answers in the fetched HTML, for example “Which areas do you cover?” and “Will you need planning permission for any of our services?”. Answers appear to load only when a question is opened.

Why it matters

Search engines and AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) mostly read the HTML the server sends. Questions such as “do I need planning permission for a tiled conservatory roof” are high-intent searches CRI could be answering, but the answers are invisible to them.

Recommended fix

Render every answer on the server inside a native <details> element, which stays collapsible without JavaScript. Note that Google now shows FAQ rich results mainly for government and health sites, so do this for visibility in normal results and AI answers, not for the FAQ snippet.

#11 Medium No price guidance anywhere UX & conversionEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

No prices, ranges or “from” figures on any page. Competitor lmdconservatoryroofs.co.uk states on its homepage that replacement roofs start from around £5,500.

Why it matters

Cost is the first question every buyer has. A “typical range” pre-qualifies enquiries, cuts time spent quoting people who can’t afford it, and keeps visitors from leaving to find a competitor who will tell them.

Recommended fix

Add a “What does it cost?” section with real ranges by conservatory size for each service (figures to be supplied by the client), plus the factors that move the price.
#12 Medium Service pages are thin and reuse the homepage images On-page SEOEffort: MediumConfirmed

Evidence

/services/tiled-conservatory-roof is seven bullets, an options list and a timeframe; it reuses the same before/after pair as the homepage. Neither service page covers cost, the installation process, materials or tile system, building regulations, or reviews about that service. The pages do contain strong specifics worth building on: U-value 0.15 versus 0.18, and 3–5 days versus 1 day.

Why it matters

These two pages should rank for the money searches (“tiled conservatory roof”, “conservatory roof insulation”). Competing pages answer the buyer’s questions in one place; thin pages give Google little to rank and the buyer little reason to call.

Recommended fix

Expand each to roughly 800–1,200 words: how it works step by step, materials and brand, building regulations and planning, what affects cost, a gallery from that service only, three reviews that mention it, and the relevant FAQs answered in full.
#13 Medium No structured data describing the business or its services Technical SEOEffort: LowClient-reported

Evidence

Reported in your brief; consistent with the rest of the head being minimal (no canonical or Open Graph tags detected). My fetch tool doesn’t expose JSON-LD, so confirm with Google’s Rich Results Test.

Why it matters

Markup confirms the business entity (name, phone, area served, founding date, social profiles) to Google and AI agents. Be careful when pitching this: Google doesn’t show review stars for a business’s reviews of itself on LocalBusiness markup, so the benefit is understanding and eligibility, not instant stars.

Recommended fix

Add HomeAndConstructionBusiness markup site-wide, Service markup on each service page and BreadcrumbList on inner pages. Confirm the address details with the client before publishing.
#14 Medium No canonical tags detected Technical SEOEffort: LowLikely

Evidence

The fetch returned no canonical link for any CRI page, while the same fetch returned canonicals for njnconservatories.co.uk and lmdconservatoryroofs.co.uk. The competitor links in your brief carry ?utm_source=chatgpt.com; links to CRI will pick up the same parameters.

Why it matters

Without a canonical, every tracked or parameterised link can be indexed as a separate copy of the page, splitting ranking signals.

Recommended fix

Set metadataBase once in the root layout and a self-referencing alternates.canonical in each page’s metadata (or in generateMetadata() for CMS routes).
#15 Medium No Open Graph or Twitter card tags Technical SEOEffort: LowLikely

Evidence

No og: or twitter: tags were detected on any page. Both competitors fetched have og:title, og:image and twitter:card. Client also reports this.

Why it matters

Half of CRI’s reviews come from Facebook, and word of mouth travels by WhatsApp. When someone shares the site there, the preview has no controlled image or headline, so it looks unfinished next to competitors’ link previews.

Recommended fix

Add site-wide Open Graph defaults with a 1200×630 before/after image, and per-page titles and descriptions.
#16 Medium No XML sitemap (robots.txt unchecked) Technical SEOEffort: LowClient-reported

Evidence

Reported in your brief. My fetch tool can only open URLs linked from pages, so I couldn’t check /sitemap.xml or /robots.txt directly; neither is linked anywhere.

Why it matters

With only seven pages Google will find them anyway, but a sitemap becomes important once area pages and project pages are added, and it is how you see indexing problems in Search Console.

Recommended fix

Generate both from Next.js using Sanity data, then submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Adjust the GROQ query to the real schema type names.
#17 Medium The hero leads with the brand name instead of the outcome UX & conversionEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

Homepage hero: heading “CRI West Midlands”, a 30-word subheading, a “SCROLL DOWN” label, two quote buttons and no phone number. Hero image alt text is “image for CRI West Midlands”.

Why it matters

Visitors decide within a few seconds whether a site solves their problem. The current hero makes them read a long sentence to find out.

#19 Medium Some “see the review” links point to the wrong review or a fragile search URL UX & conversionEffort: LowConfirmed

On /reviews, the reviews by Melanie and Corinne both link to the same Google share link (share.google/xWQcfCsW3bykgFA8l). The “See our google reviews” button links to a copied Chrome search URL that includes personal browser parameters (rlz=1C5CHFA_enGB1172GB1172, gs_lcrp=…).

#20 Medium Every photo is resized and compressed twice PerformanceEffort: LowConfirmed

Evidence

Every content image uses /_next/image?url=cdn.sanity.io/…?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&w=3840&q=75. Sanity resizes and compresses (quality 80), then the Next.js optimiser decodes and re-encodes again (quality 75). Whether a correct sizes attribute is set needs the raw srcset to confirm.

Why it matters

Two lossy passes visibly soften photos, which matters for a business whose sales pitch is the finished look. The second pass also adds server work and counts against the Vercel image-optimisation allowance.

Recommended fix

Use a custom loader so Sanity’s CDN does the resizing once, and set sizes on every image so phones download phone-sized files.
#21 Medium Projects page is an unlabelled photo grid with no case studies On-page SEOEffort: MediumConfirmed

Evidence

/projects shows 30 images with no captions, links or detail pages. The filter (All / Interior Roof Insulation / Conservatory Roof Tiling) is not made of links, so filtered views can’t be shared or indexed.

Why it matters

Each project is a ready-made local case study (“Tiled roof in Kingswinford, 4 days”). As pictures only, they carry no searchable text and can’t support the area pages.

Recommended fix

Give each project a page (for example /projects/kingswinford-tiled-roof) with town, roof type, duration, before/after photos and the matching review. Link projects from area and service pages.
#22 Medium Reviews and Projects pages carry a large number of photos PerformanceEffort: MediumNeeds data

Evidence

/reviews embeds about 58 review photos plus platform icons; /projects embeds 30. Lazy loading and total transfer size couldn’t be checked without PageSpeed or raw HTML.

Why it matters

If more than the first screenful loads up front, these pages will be slow on mobile data, and these are exactly the pages a sceptical buyer opens.

#23 Medium The privacy policy is a generic template Security & best practicesEffort: MediumConfirmed

Evidence

/policies/privacy-policy (updated 5 March 2026) talks about using details to ship an order and emailing about specials and new products. It doesn’t name the business as data controller or give its address, and doesn’t mention lawful basis, retention periods, the right to complain to the ICO, or the services that process form data (form provider, Vercel, Sanity, any analytics).

Why it matters

UK GDPR requires clear transparency information when collecting personal data through the quote form. A template that refers to shipping orders also undermines credibility. This is a flag for review, not legal advice.

Recommended fix

Rewrite it against the ICO’s guidance for small businesses, listing each processor and the actual purposes (quotes, surveys, follow-up). Have the client confirm the details.
#24 Medium AI assistants and browsing agents can’t complete “get me a quote” Technical SEOEffort: MediumLikely

Evidence

Combines confirmed findings: the quote form has no URL, FAQ answers are not in the HTML, phone and email aren’t links, and structured data is reported absent. You mention a low “agent browsing” score; I don’t know which tool produced it, so I can’t verify the number.

Why it matters

People increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity to “find a conservatory roof company near Stourbridge and get me a quote”. Agents need a findable URL, labelled form fields and machine-readable contact details to do that.

Recommended fix

Most of this is solved by the quote page, server-rendered FAQs, tel/mailto links and structured data. Also use a semantic <form> with a <label> on every field and an <address> block. An /llms.txt summary is cheap to add but is an emerging convention, not a confirmed ranking factor.
#25 Medium Header button text is duplicated in the markup AccessibilityEffort: LowLikely

Evidence

Each header CTA outputs “Get a quote” and “Get a free quote” back to back, and the pair appears twice per page. If the hidden variant is hidden with opacity, transform or width rather than display: none, screen readers announce both.

Why it matters

Repeated, run-together labels make the main action harder to understand for assistive-technology users (WCAG 2.5.3 Label in Name, 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value).

Recommended fix

Render a single label, or hide the alternative at each breakpoint with display: none. Verify with axe DevTools or VoiceOver.
#26 Medium Company details are not shown (may be a legal requirement) Security & best practicesEffort: LowNeeds data

Evidence

The Yell listing linked from /reviews is titled “cri-west-midlands-ltd-stourbridge”, which suggests a limited company. The site shows no legal name, company number or registered office. I couldn’t confirm the company on Companies House.

Why it matters

If the business is a limited company, UK trading-disclosure rules require the registered name, number, place of registration and registered office on its website. Showing them, plus a VAT number if registered, is also a trust signal (NJN shows its VAT number).

Recommended fix

Confirm the legal status, then add a footer line such as “CRI West Midlands Ltd, registered in England and Wales no. ________, registered office ________”.
